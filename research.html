Different methods.
Local questions.

A plain-language introduction to several ways people grow food and integrate farm activities. These are broad methods, not guarantees of results or recommendations for a particular site. Suitability depends on local conditions, people, costs and markets.

How to use this page: Treat it as a starting point for learning, not as a technical manual. The methods below are general concepts. This page does not profile or imply affiliation with any particular organisation or practitioner.

Six approaches to explore

Understand the idea—and its trade-offs

Methods can be combined, but they are not interchangeable. Begin with the purpose of the system, the site's constraints and the people who will operate it.

🐟

Aquaculture

Raising aquatic organisms

Aquaculture is the cultivation of aquatic organisms such as fish, shellfish or aquatic plants in managed freshwater or marine environments. Systems range from ponds and tanks to cages and recirculating setups.

Questions to explore

  • Is water quality and supply appropriate and dependable?
  • What are the species, feed, energy, health-management and market requirements?
  • How will waste, escapes, disease risks and animal welfare be managed?
🏡

Polyhouse cultivation

Protected growing

A polyhouse uses a covered structure to modify growing conditions and protect crops from some weather and pest pressures. It may support more controlled production, but does not remove climate, disease, water or market risks.

Questions to explore

  • Which crop and growing season fit the local climate and buyers?
  • What are construction, repair, irrigation, ventilation and labour costs?
  • How will temperature, humidity, pests and diseases be monitored?
💧

Hydroponics

Soilless plant growing

Hydroponics grows plants with roots supplied by a managed nutrient solution, sometimes using an inert growing medium. System types differ in water flow, aeration and equipment needs.

Questions to explore

  • Are water quality, nutrients, power and technical skills available?
  • What happens during equipment failure or supply interruptions?
  • Do expected crop prices justify investment and operating costs?
🌳

Multi-layer farming

Planning vertical space

Multi-layer farming arranges crops with different heights, canopy shapes, root patterns or growing periods in the same area. The design must account for light, water, nutrients, access, harvest timing and competition.

Questions to explore

  • Are the selected crops compatible in this site's soil and climate?
  • Will shade, root competition or harvest access limit production?
  • Can the mix provide a practical harvest and market through the year?
🐄

Integrated farming with animals

Connecting farm activities

Integrated farming combines crops with one or more activities such as livestock, poultry, fish or trees. By-products from one activity may be useful to another when safely and appropriately managed; integration needs careful planning, not assumptions of a closed or waste-free system.

Questions to explore

  • Can the farm provide suitable feed, water, care and veterinary support?
  • How will manure, nutrients, biosecurity and food safety be managed?
  • Do the added activities fit available labour, skills and markets?
🧭

Permaculture

Whole-site design approach

Permaculture is a design approach that considers relationships among people, plants, animals, buildings, water and land. Designs are adapted to a site's ecology and the needs of the people managing it; the label alone does not establish a particular outcome.

Questions to explore

  • What are the site’s water flows, soil conditions, sunlight and existing assets?
  • Which design choices reduce avoidable work while meeting real needs?
  • How will outcomes, costs and maintenance be observed over time?

Before choosing a method

Start with the questions that matter locally

PlaceWhat do soil, water, climate and available space allow?
PeopleWho will do the work, and what skills or support are needed?
EconomicsWhat are the full setup, operating and maintenance costs?
MarketWho will buy the output, at what quality and price?
RisksWhat can fail, and what is the plan for disruption?
EvidenceHow will results be measured and compared fairly?

Be.Live learning position: These methods are subjects for study and discussion, not promises of yield, income, health benefits or environmental impact. Any future experiment should be small enough to learn from, planned with the people involved, and evaluated against local evidence.
